Honestly, I was close to burnout last year. But the strange thing was — my mind wasn't empty. It was the opposite.

I was in a constant state of hyperarousal. My brain was always on. It wasn't that I didn't know what to do. The problem was that I couldn't stop.

A new model drops on X. I need to check it. A new framework is trending on GitHub. I need to test it. If I don't, I'll fall behind. Everyone else is probably already using it. I need to adopt it fast.

That's the real problem with information overload in the AI era. It doesn't end at reading. When you read something, you feel like you have to act on it. When you don't act, you feel anxious. When you feel anxious, you read more. The loop never closes.

This continued right up until the moment I fell asleep. Even when I was resting, I wasn't really resting.

---

## What I did

At some point, I stopped going directly to the platforms.

I set up an AI agent to filter and deliver only what I needed to see. Key accounts on X, GitHub Trending, GeekNews — instead of going in myself, I get a curated summary once a day. Same with Slack. I stopped living in the channels and started responding only when something relevant came through.

Everyone knows that once you open X, it's hard to stop. That it's designed that way. So I blocked the entrance entirely.

---

## What I got back

Happiness came back.

When talking with my team, I started feeling a sense of accomplishment again. The feeling of actually building something together — of shared momentum. These were sensations I had lost while just running.

And the most important thing — my family.

When I was living in hyperarousal, I was physically present but mentally somewhere else. My body was home, but my mind was still racing. That changed. It sounds small, but honestly, it's everything.

---

## The irony of the AI era

You can't slow down the pace. That's just true. New technologies keep coming, competition doesn't stop.

But to survive inside that pace — you need a solid personal routine. When to look, when to stop, what to filter out. Without that, the speed consumes you.

Sustainability comes from routine. And routine comes from choice. Not the choice to consume more, but the choice to consume less.

It's still ironic that in the AI era, the way to use AI better turned out to be using AI to filter out everything AI was producing.

---

*Written on a day when I actually got enough sleep.*
